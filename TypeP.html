01 · Telemetry source

A WATCH CAN REMEMBER.
BUT CAN IT BE TRUSTED?

Wearable devices can record movement, time and physiological signals that may support forensic event reconstruction. However, recorded telemetry is not automatically trustworthy and should be validated before its evidentiary reliability is assessed.

Forensic limitations
Sensor noise
Motion artifacts can distort physiological measurements.
Clock drift
Independent device clocks may not share an identical temporal baseline.
Proprietary processing
Vendor-side filtering can affect how recorded signals are represented.
Telemetry gaps
Missing or incomplete records can weaken a timeline.
02 · The forensic problem

RAW TELEMETRY ≠
AUTOMATICALLY RELIABLE EVIDENCE

A single data stream can contain artifacts, missing information or timing uncertainty. DVTF therefore treats telemetry as information that must be assessed and cross-verified—not as unquestioned proof.

03 · Core research hypothesis
H1

Multi-sensor cross-verification will improve the reliability of forensic event reconstruction compared with single-source telemetry analysis.

H0 — No meaningful improvement.

Stage 01 → Stage 02

Preserve first. Interpret second.

The proposed workflow begins with documented acquisition and preservation. It then asks a basic scientific question: is the recorded signal of sufficient quality to interpret?

Conceptual workflow
Stage 03 · Alignment mechanism

Temporal normalization + cross-modal convergence

Independent streams are placed on a unified temporal baseline and compared for agreement or divergence. Agreement can strengthen confidence; disagreement triggers further evaluation.
