01 • Telemetry Source

A WATCH CAN REMEMBER.
BUT CAN IT BE TRUSTED?

Wearable devices continuously log physical movement, temporal milestones, and physiological signatures that can assist in forensic event reconstruction.

However, recorded telemetry is not inherently authoritative. Before data can be evaluated as trustworthy evidence, its technical integrity and contextual validity must be rigorously verified.

DATA CORRUPTION FACTORS
  • SENSOR NOISE: Optical PPG distortion from motion artifacts.
  • CLOCK DRIFT: Desynchronization between device internal RTC and UTC.
  • PROPRIETARY SMOOTHING: Vendor OS filtering altering raw peaks.
  • TELEMETRY GAP: Aggressive power-saving drops vital data points.
02 • Forensic Limitation

RAW TELEMETRY ≠
AUTOMATICALLY RELIABLE EVIDENCE

Single-source sensor logs are frequently presented as objective truth in legal contexts. However, hardware constraints and algorithmic interpolation can introduce significant anomalies.

Without systematic cross-verification, isolated data points run the risk of misinterpreting ordinary biological or mechanical artifacts as critical forensic events.

03 • Core Research Hypothesis

"Multi-sensor cross-verification will improve the reliability of forensic event reconstruction compared with single-source telemetry analysis."

H1 — PRIMARY HYPOTHESIS

Cross-modal alignment of PPG and kinetic sensors reduces false temporal classification errors in digital event timelines.

H0 — NULL HYPOTHESIS

Multi-sensor cross-verification yields no statistically meaningful improvement over isolated single-stream forensic logs.
